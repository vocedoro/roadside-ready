How to choose

1. Amps: maintain or recharge?

For pure maintenance, 1 to 2 amps is plenty. If you also want to recharge a flat car battery in a reasonable time, choose around 5 amps; 10 amps suits large truck, RV or marine batteries.

2. Battery type

Most cars use flooded lead-acid or AGM batteries. Some motorcycles, boats and RVs use lithium (LiFePO4). Check that the charger lists your battery type. Some excellent chargers are lead-acid / AGM only.

3. Temperature compensation

Batteries need slightly different charging voltage in cold and heat. Chargers with a temperature sensor adjust automatically, which helps in an unheated garage.

4. Reconditioning and dead batteries

Many smart chargers refuse to charge a battery below a certain voltage. Models that start from very low voltage, or offer a manual force mode, can sometimes recover a battery that others will not. A battery that is old or damaged still needs replacing.

Safety: follow the connection order in your charger's manual, charge flooded batteries in a ventilated space, and never charge a frozen battery.

FAQ

Can I leave a maintainer connected all winter?

Smart maintainers are designed for that; they switch to a maintenance mode when the battery is full. Check the cables and battery occasionally anyway.

Maintainer or jump starter?

Different jobs. A maintainer prevents a dead battery at home; a jump starter rescues you on the road. Our winter car emergency kit checklist covers jump starters.
